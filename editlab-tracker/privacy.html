Privacy Policy

EditLab Tracker, and the EditLab Tracker connector for the Claude app · last updated 31 October 2026

The short version

EditLab Tracker does not collect any data. Nothing you do in the app is sent to us. There are no analytics, no telemetry, no accounts, no advertising and no third-party SDKs of any kind. The one way your song leaves your Mac is the Claude link below, and only once you turn it on.

What the app stores, and where

Everything the app keeps stays on your own device:

  • Songs you save are written to files you choose, in the location you choose, using the standard macOS save panel. They are .eltrk files, which are plain JSON.
  • Audio you import — records, samples — is read from files you choose, and is stored inside your song file. It is never uploaded.
  • Preferences and recovery data are kept in the app’s own sandboxed storage on your device, so that unsaved work can be offered back to you after an unexpected quit.

None of this leaves your device, and none of it is visible to the developer.

Claude, if you link it

Tracker can work with the Claude app for Mac, through the EditLab Tracker connector that comes inside the app. The link is off until you choose Help > Use EditLab Tracker with Claude…, add the connector in the Claude app, and click Allow when Tracker asks.

  • What Claude gets. While the link is on, Claude can read the song open in Tracker, and what Tracker tells it about the song: its name, tempo and play order, its channels and notes, the names and lengths of its sounds, the mixer’s settings, the steps in Edit > Undo, and measurements Tracker takes of how part of the song sounds, such as its level, key and tuning. The song’s audio itself is not sent. What Claude reads becomes part of your Claude chat, and is handled by Anthropic under your agreement with them: anthropic.com/legal/privacy.
  • The connector. It runs on your Mac and talks only to Tracker, over a private connection that stays on your Mac (127.0.0.1). It collects nothing, and sends nothing anywhere else. The first time it connects, it makes a random key and keeps it on your Mac, so that Tracker can tell it is the connector you allowed.
  • What we get. Nothing. The link runs between Tracker and the Claude app, on your Mac; nothing goes to EditLab.
  • Ask Claude. A request you type into Tracker’s ask bar and send with Ask Claude opens in a new chat in the Claude app, and nothing is sent until you send it there.
  • Taking it back. Everything Claude changes is a normal edit you can take back with Edit > Undo, and files are saved or exported only through Tracker’s own Save and Export windows, which you confirm. Help > Turn Off the Claude Link turns the link off, and you can remove the connector in the Claude app’s settings.

Network access

The app makes no requests to the internet. Its interface is loaded from files inside the application bundle, and a Content-Security-Policy restricts it to the bundle’s own origin. If you turn on the Claude link, Tracker also accepts connections from the connector on your own Mac (127.0.0.1) and nothing else. There is no server component to this app.

Artificial intelligence features

The app’s assistant runs entirely on your device using Apple’s FoundationModels framework. What you type into it is processed locally and is not transmitted to the developer, to Apple, or to any other party. The models that take the vocal and the instruments apart when you split a song also run on your Mac. Claude is used only through the link above, if you turn it on.

Effect Designs

You can load an Effect Design — an .eltrkfx file — to add an effect. These are JSON documents describing how audio nodes are wired. They are read from files you choose on your own device, and the app makes no network request to fetch or run one.

Children

The app is a music tool and does not collect data from anyone, including children.
